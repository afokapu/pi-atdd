Title: Give each component exactly one business capability
Given: A planner artifact declares, describes or references a component (new or existing)
When: the planner defines or evaluates that component's responsibility
Then: Ensure the component has exactly one business capability.
Prefer:
- Pick the single capability from the canonical vocabulary a component name can express — core business: validator (validates business rules and constraints), processor (processes business logic and computations), coordinator (coordinates multiple operations), transformer (transforms data between formats), aggregator (aggregates data from multiple sources); UI/UX: display (displays information to users), collector (collects user input), navigator (manages navigation flow); integration: persister (persists data to storage), fetcher (fetches data from external sources), publisher (publishes events/notifications), subscriber (subscribes to events/notifications).
- Compliant names: option-validator, decision-processor, dilemma-display, choice-persister, result-publisher.
Avoid:
- Components whose responsibility spans several business capabilities, or that hide the mix behind a generic word.
- REQUIRED, not optional: a component name that does not reflect its primary business purpose. Check this separately from the single-capability check; passing one does not pass the other.
- REQUIRED, not optional: a component name that does not follow the pattern {business-capability}-{layer-role}. A name that reflects the purpose but breaks the pattern is still non-compliant.
- Vague catch-all names with no single business capability, such as manager-helper-util.
Why: One business capability per component keeps responsibilities sharp and makes the component's name a faithful index of what it does, so the graph stays readable and components stay swappable.

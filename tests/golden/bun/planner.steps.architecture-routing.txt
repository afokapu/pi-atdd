Title: Route each JTBD step to its layer(s) and canonical call sequence
Given: A JTBD step (define, locate, prepare, confirm, execute, monitor, modify, resolve, conclude) appears in work being planned, in any artifact, with or without existing routing
When: the step is declared, assigned or reviewed
Then: Declare for every JTBD step its primary architecture layer(s) and its canonical ordered call sequence of layer.operation tokens across presentation, application, domain and integration, exactly as in the canonical step table.
Prefer:
- define [domain]: presentation.command → application.use_case → domain.create → integration.persist
- locate [application]: application.query → integration.read → application.aggregate → presentation.display
- prepare [application]: application.use_case → domain.validate → integration.read → application.stage
- confirm [application]: application.query → integration.read → domain.validate → application.decide
- execute [domain, application]: presentation.command → application.use_case → domain.mutate → integration.persist
- monitor [presentation]: presentation.query → application.query → integration.read → application.aggregate → presentation.display
- modify [application]: presentation.command → application.use_case → domain.mutate → integration.persist
- resolve [application]: application.event → application.use_case → domain.policy → integration.call_external → integration.persist
- conclude [integration, application]: application.use_case → domain.validate → integration.persist → integration.archive
- Write sequences as ordered layer.operation tokens so steps are mechanically routable.
Avoid:
- Leaving any step without primary layer(s) or call sequence.
- Reordering, dropping or inventing tokens versus the step's canonical sequence.
- Layer names outside presentation, application, domain, integration.
- Adding extra steps to the taxonomy to fit a sequence instead of routing via the table.
Why: Binding steps to layers and call sequences keeps the JTBD taxonomy minimal yet routable, so a step can be mechanically placed into the layered architecture.

Title: Scope each train to one coherent journey
Given: A plan contains a train and its wagons, being authored, edited, or reviewed (including an unchanged existing train or a wagon change that may alter the train's journey).
When: The planner sets or checks the train's outcome and which wagons it contains.
Then: The train must tell ONE coherent journey: if its outcome cannot be stated in one sentence without 'and', split it.
Prefer:
- Size: 3-7 wagons is the recommended sweet spot; 2 is the allowed minimum.
- At 8-12 wagons, review whether the train holds multiple journeys (split if it does); at 13+, decompose.
- Treat these as decomposition signals to investigate (do they mean multiple journeys?): outcome with multiple 'AND's; both setup AND teardown of a lifecycle; mix of exploratory (gesture) and core-mechanic flows; multiple independent feedback loops; participants from 3+ themes.
Avoid:
- Trains of 13+ wagons (antipattern), e.g. a 16-wagon 'complete lifecycle' train spanning multiple journeys.
- Single-wagon trains (below the minimum of 2).
Why: A train demonstrates one user journey end to end; bundling journeys hides independent outcomes and makes the train too large to review and accept as one story.

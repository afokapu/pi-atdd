Title: Bind a browser spec to every human-facing train
Given: A human-facing train (one with a `user:` participant) is declared under `plan/`.
When: The tester writes or reviews browser coverage for declared trains.
Then: Ensure at least one browser spec is bound to that train by a `// Train: <train id>` header.
Prefer:
- Add `e2e/<train-slug>.e2e.ts` with `// Train: <train id>`, then make it prove the train, not merely name it.
- Claim: read the declared train/journey behavior; identify the user-observable outcome it should deliver.
- Stimulus: enter through the declared user-facing entrypoint; perform the real browser interactions that exercise the train.
- Witness: assert the final user-visible business outcome, not only navigation, a 200 response, or an internal state token.
Avoid:
- Adding the Train header solely for coverage credit.
- Calling an internal handler instead of walking the assembled journey.
- Seeding the final result directly.
- Asserting only that the page loaded while the train behavior stays unobserved.
- Counting a unit test that merely mentions a journey URN in a string as a browser spec.
- Treating structural coverage as proof: a spec that only binds the train, navigates successfully, or mentions its identifier can pass coverage while the journey behavior stays unproven.
Unless:
- Trains in which no person takes part (only wagons and systems) have no screen to drive and are out of scope.
Why: A train with no browser spec is a silent gap: its journey can regress with nothing turning red. `// Train:` is only the coverage handle, not the proof — binding, navigating, or naming the train satisfies coverage yet leaves behavior unproven.

Title: Declare journeys as topology above interlockings: entrypoint, continuations, explicit terminals
Given: A journey document composing routing decisions across interlockings, each interlocking selecting exactly one linear train
When: the planner authors or changes the journey's entrypoint, routes, continuations or terminals
Then: Declare the journey as {station-entrypoint, entrypoint-interlocking, route-continuations, explicit-terminals}: if exposed, its starting Station Master actions, consumer surfaces and first interlocking; and for every route, after its selected train completes, either an explicit terminal or a continuation from (interlocking_id, route_id) via a named artifact that train produces to the next interlocking.
Prefer:
- Compose interlocking -> train -> interlocking; keep each interlocking a local boundary selecting one linear train.
- Ownership: Interlocking selects the train, TrainRunner executes wagons linearly, Journey owns continuation.
- Exposed journeys: Bun binds the declared Station Master actions to {journeyId, path} via coder.bun.station-master-journey-routing.
- Treat journey artifacts as planner data; a separate Bun runtime may introduce a JourneyRunner.
Avoid:
- Recursive interlockings (one selecting or invoking another).
- Moving wagon execution out of TrainRunner, or letting any of the three absorb the others.
- Implicitly dangling routes, or continuations not keyed by an artifact the selected train produces.
- Encoding runtime execution (e.g. a JourneyRunner) into the semantic journey definition.
Why: Keeping continuation in the journey lets routing compose across interlockings without recursive interlockings or split wagon execution, so each boundary stays local and verifiable.
